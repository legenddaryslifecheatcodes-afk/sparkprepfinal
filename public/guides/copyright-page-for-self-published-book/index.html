Your book's copyright page: what to put on it, in plain English

Updated November 26, 2026 · Any self-published book (U.S. focus)

The copyright page is the small page right after the title page, full of fine print. It's not hard to write, and you don't need a lawyer for a basic one. Here's what goes on it.

What goes on a copyright page in a self-published book? A basic copyright page has the copyright line (© 2026 Your Name), "All rights reserved", your ISBN for that format, the edition, and a contact or website. Add a fiction disclaimer if it's a novel. Your book is copyrighted the moment you write it; registering is optional and costs $45 online for one author's single work.

Where it goes

On the back of the title page (the left-hand page right after it). In an ebook it's often moved to the end of the book.

What to include

  • The copyright line: Copyright © 2026 by Your Name (or your pen name).
  • "All rights reserved" plus a short sentence saying no part may be copied without permission, except short quotes in reviews.
  • The ISBN for this format. Paperback, hardcover and ebook each have their own (see do I need an ISBN?).
  • Edition: First edition, 2026.
  • Publisher or imprint name, if you have one, and a website or email.
  • Credits: cover designer, editor, photo credits.
  • For fiction: a line saying names and events are made up and any likeness to real people is a coincidence.

A simple example

Copyright © 2026 by Jane Author
All rights reserved. No part of this book may be reproduced in any form without written permission from the author, except for brief quotations in a book review.
This is a work of fiction. Names, characters and events are products of the author's imagination.
ISBN 978-0-000000-00-0 (paperback)
First edition, 2026
Cover design by Name · janeauthor.com

The ISBN above is a placeholder. Use your real one, and make sure it matches the barcode on your cover; a mismatch gets books rejected (how to fix it).

Do you need to register your copyright?

In the U.S., your book is protected by copyright as soon as you write it down. Registering with the U.S. Copyright Office is optional, but it gives you a public record and is needed before you can sue someone for copying your book in a U.S. court.

  • Online, one author, one work, not made for hire: $45
  • Standard online application: $65

Fees from the U.S. Copyright Office fee page, checked November 2026. The Office says its fees change on January 5, 2027, so check before you file. This is general information, not legal advice.

Formatting tips

  • Use a smaller font than your story (but at least 7 point, KDP's minimum).
  • Keep it inside the same margins as every other page (margin chart).
  • No page number is usually shown on this page.

Quick answers

Do I need a copyright page in my book?

It isn't legally required, but every professional book has one. It states who owns the book, the ISBN and the edition, and stores and libraries expect it.

How much does it cost to copyright a book?

Your book is copyrighted for free the moment you write it. Registering it with the U.S. Copyright Office costs $45 online for one author's single work, or $65 for the standard online application (fees checked November 2026).

Can I use a pen name on my copyright page?

Yes. Many authors put their pen name in the copyright line. The U.S. Copyright Office also lets you register under a pseudonym.
