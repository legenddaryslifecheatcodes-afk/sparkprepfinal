How to fix a Canva PDF that's rejected for color

Updated November 16, 2026 · Amazon KDP · IngramSpark

Canva is the go-to for book covers. But its files start in screen color, and that trips up printers.

Why you get “File contains RGB color”

Canva designs in RGB, the color of screens. Printers use CMYK ink. When RGB black is turned into ink, it often becomes a "rich black" that mixes all four inks.

That causes two problems. A black-and-white interior is only allowed black ink, so any color fails. And dark areas can pass IngramSpark's 240% total ink limit, which gets the file rejected.

How to fix “File contains RGB color” manually (the hard way)

If you want to try adjusting the files yourself, here are the steps you need to follow:

  1. In Canva, choose Share → Download → PDF Print.
  2. Turn on "Crop marks and bleed" only if your printer asks for bleed in the file, and pick CMYK under Color profile if your plan shows it.
  3. Make black text and lines plain black, not a dark photo or gradient.
  4. Open the PDF in Acrobat Pro and check Print Production → Output Preview for RGB and for areas over 240% total ink.
  5. Re-upload it to your printer and wait for the file review again. If it fails, you start over.

Quick answers

Is Canva's PDF Print good enough for KDP?

It can be, if the size and bleed are right. Color is the usual problem, so check for RGB and rich black before uploading.

What is rich black?

Black made from several inks mixed together instead of black ink alone. It can break ink limits and isn't allowed in black-and-white books.
