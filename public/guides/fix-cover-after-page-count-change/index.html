Why you get “Cover spine width does not match the page count of the interior”

Your spine width comes from your page count and your paper. Add pages and the spine gets wider. Cut pages and it gets thinner. The full cover width changes with it.

So a cover made for 220 pages no longer fits the same book at 260 pages. The spine text drifts off the spine, and the printer's size check rejects the file. Changing from white to cream paper does the same thing.

How to fix “Cover spine width does not match the page count of the interior” manually (the hard way)

If you want to try adjusting the files yourself, here are the steps you need to follow:

  1. Note your new final page count and your paper (white or cream).
  2. Get the new full cover size and spine width from your printer's own calculator or template.
  3. Change your cover document to the new full size.
  4. Move the front to the right edge, re-center the spine text on the new spine, and keep the back in place. Re-export as a print PDF.
  5. Re-upload it to your printer and wait for the file review again. If it fails, you start over.

The most common mistake: Only widening the spine strip in the middle and forgetting that the front cover has to move right by the same amount.

How to check it's fixed before you upload again

  • Your file's full width matches the new size from your printer's own calculator for the new page count and paper.
  • The spine text is centered on the new spine with room on both sides.
  • The page count in your book setup matches the real page count of your interior PDF.

Quick answers

Do I need a new cover if I add pages?

You need a resized cover. The front and back art can stay the same, but the spine and full width change.

How many pages can I add before the cover is wrong?

Any change in page count changes the spine width, so get a new size from your printer's calculator every time.
