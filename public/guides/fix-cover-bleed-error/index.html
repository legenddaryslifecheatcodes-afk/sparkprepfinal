How to fix the "insufficient or no bleed" cover error

Updated November 20, 2026 · Amazon KDP · IngramSpark · Barnes & Noble Press · Lulu

Bleed is the most common cover rejection there is, and one of the easiest to fix once you know what the printer wants.

What is bleed in printing? Bleed in printing is extra artwork that runs past the edge where the page will be cut. For books, it's 0.125 inch (3.2 mm) on each outside edge. Printers trim the sheet after printing, and the cut can drift slightly, so bleed stops thin white lines from showing at the edges.

Why you get “INSUFFICIENT OR NO BLEED ON COVER LAYOUT”

Printers print your cover on a bigger sheet and then cut it down. The blade can land a tiny bit off. Bleed is extra background art, 0.125" (3.2 mm) past every edge, that gets cut away. If your art stops exactly at the edge, a slightly-off cut leaves a thin white line.

So printers check that your background runs all the way past the trim line. If it doesn't, or if the file is the trim size instead of the bigger bleed size, the cover is rejected.

How to fix “INSUFFICIENT OR NO BLEED ON COVER LAYOUT” manually (the hard way)

If you want to try adjusting the files yourself, here are the steps you need to follow:

  1. Open your original cover file (Canva, InDesign, Photoshop, Word or whatever you designed it in).
  2. Change the document size to the full size with bleed: add 0.125" to every outside edge, so 0.25" more in total to both the width and the height.
  3. Drag every background photo and color block out past the edge, to the bleed line. Keep all text well inside the trim line.
  4. Re-export as a print PDF (PDF/X-1a:2001 if your program has it) with the bleed included and no crop marks or printer marks.
  5. Re-upload it to your printer and wait for the file review again. If it fails, you start over.

Step by step in your app

Work out the right size first

  1. Full cover width = 0.125" + back cover + spine + front cover + 0.125".
  2. Full cover height = 0.125" + book height + 0.125". A 6" x 9" book is 9.25" tall.
  3. Use the KDP cover calculator or your printer's own template to get the exact numbers.

Canva

  1. Create a custom design at the full cover size from the step above.
  2. Turn on File → View settings → Show print bleed so you can see the cut line.
  3. Stretch every background photo and color block past the outer edge.
  4. Download as PDF Print with the bleed included. Don't add crop marks: printers don't want them in the file.

Adobe InDesign

  1. File → Document Setup → Bleed and Slug: set 0.125 in on all sides.
  2. Drag background art to the red bleed line on every edge.
  3. File → Export → Adobe PDF (Print), and under Marks and Bleeds tick "Use Document Bleed Settings".

Check it in Acrobat

  1. Open the PDF and look at the page size (File → Properties). It should be the full size with bleed, not the trim size.
  2. Zoom into each corner: the background should keep going right to the edge of the page.

The most common mistake: Making the cover file the exact trim size and then stretching the art. The page itself has to be bigger, not just the picture.

How to check it's fixed before you upload again

  • In Acrobat (File, Properties), the page size is the full size with bleed: for a 6" x 9" paperback the height is 9.25".
  • Zoom into all four corners. The background color or photo runs right to the edge of the page with no white sliver.
  • No crop marks, printer marks or white border were added when you exported.

Quick answers

What is a bleed error on a book cover?

A bleed error means your cover's background stops right at the edge instead of running 0.125" past it. Printers trim the edge off, so without bleed you can get a thin white line. Fix it by making the cover file at bleed size and stretching the background art past every edge.

What does full bleed mean in printing?

Full bleed means the artwork runs all the way to every edge of the finished page or cover, with no white border. To print full bleed, your file must be 0.125" bigger than the book on every outside edge, with the background reaching that outer edge.

How much bleed does a book cover need?

0.125 inches (3.2 mm) on every outside edge for paperbacks at KDP, IngramSpark, Barnes & Noble Press and Lulu. Hardcover case covers need more, so always use your printer's own template for hardcovers.

Should text go into the bleed?

No. Only background art goes into the bleed. Keep titles and names well inside the trim line so they never get cut.
