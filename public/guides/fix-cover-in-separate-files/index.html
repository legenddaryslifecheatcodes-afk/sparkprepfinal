How to fix a cover made as separate front, spine and back files

Updated November 26, 2026 · Amazon KDP · IngramSpark

Designers often hand you a front cover, a back cover and a spine as three files. A paperback printer only takes one.

Can I upload my book cover as separate front, spine and back files? No. A paperback cover prints on one sheet and wraps around the book, so the printer needs one file laid out left to right: back cover, spine, front cover, with 0.125" bleed around the outside. Uploading just the front or three separate pictures gets rejected, or the front art is stretched to fill the space.

Cover must be a single file that includes the back cover, spine and front cover

Paraphrased. Each printer words this a little differently.

Why did your file get rejected?

A paperback cover is printed on one sheet and wrapped around the book. So the printer needs one file laid out left to right: back cover, spine, front cover, with 0.125" of bleed around the outside.

If you upload just the front, or three separate pictures, there is nothing to wrap around the spine and back. The file gets rejected, or the printer's system stretches your front art to fill the space.

How to fix it manually (the hard way)

If you want to try adjusting the files yourself, here are the steps you need to follow:

  1. Get your printer's cover template for your exact trim size, page count and paper.
  2. Make a new document at the template's full size (back + spine + front + bleed).
  3. Place the back on the left, the spine in the middle and the front on the right, lining each one up with the template's guides.
  4. Hide the template layer, flatten, and export as a print PDF (PDF/X-1a:2001 if you can).
  5. Re-upload it to your printer and wait for the file review again. If it fails, you start over.

The most common mistake: Uploading only the front cover picture and expecting the printer to fill in the spine and back.

How to check it's fixed before you upload again

  • Your PDF is one page, at the template's full width (back + spine + front + bleed).
  • Back is on the left, front is on the right, and the spine text sits on the spine.
  • Art runs to every outside edge with no white sliver.
