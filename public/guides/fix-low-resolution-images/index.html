How to fix low-resolution (under 300 DPI) images in your book

Updated October 28, 2026 · Amazon KDP · IngramSpark · Barnes & Noble Press · Lulu

A picture that looks sharp on your screen can still print blurry. Printers want 300 DPI, and many programs quietly lower it.

1. The error

Image resolution is below 300 DPI

Paraphrased. KDP shows this as a low-resolution image warning in its previewer.

2. Why the printer rejected it

DPI (dots per inch) is how much detail an image has for its printed size. Screens need about 72 to 150. Print needs 300 DPI at the size the picture appears in the book.

So a 1200 x 1800 pixel photo is sharp printed at 4" x 6", but blurry stretched across a full 6" x 9" page. Word and some PDF tools also shrink images when saving, which drops a good picture below 300.

3. The do-it-yourself fix

Check the math

  1. Pixels needed = inches x 300. A full 6.125" x 9.25" cover with bleed needs about 1838 x 2775 pixels.
  2. If your image is smaller than that, get a bigger version from the original source.

Microsoft Word

  1. File → Options → Advanced → Image Size and Quality.
  2. Tick Do not compress images in file and set Default resolution to High fidelity.
  3. Re-insert the original images, then save as PDF.

Canva

  1. Download as PDF Print, not PDF Standard.
  2. Don't stretch a small photo to fill a large area.

Adobe InDesign

  1. Window → Links shows each image's Effective PPI. Aim for 300 or more.
  2. When exporting, under Compression, don't downsample below 300.

Quick answers

What DPI do I need for a printed book?

300 DPI at the size the image is printed. Lower resolution prints soft or pixelated.

How many pixels for a 6x9 book cover?

At 300 DPI, the front alone with bleed needs about 1838 by 2775 pixels. The full wrap is wider because it includes the back and spine.
