Why you get “RGB / color content found in a black & white interior”

A black and white book is printed with black ink only. But most programs save "black" text as RGB, which is a screen color made of red, green and blue light. When the printer converts it, that black turns into a mix of several inks. A colored logo, a blue link or a color photo does the same.

Printers reject it because colored ink can't go on a black-only press, and because mixed black can push total ink over the limit.

How to fix “RGB / color content found in a black & white interior” manually (the hard way)

If you want to try adjusting the files yourself, here are the steps you need to follow:

  1. Open your original manuscript.
  2. Set all text to plain black and convert every picture to grayscale.
  3. Remove any colored links, logos or highlights (blue hyperlinks are a common one).
  4. Re-export as PDF/X-1a:2001, or in Acrobat Pro use Print Production → Convert Colors → Grayscale.
  5. Re-upload it to your printer and wait for the file review again. If it fails, you start over.

Step by step in your app

Adobe Acrobat Pro (fastest)

  1. Tools → Print Production → Convert Colors.
  2. Set Conversion Profile to a grayscale profile (for example Dot Gain 15% or Gray Gamma 2.2).
  3. Tick Convert Colors to Output Intent if shown, then OK, and save as a new PDF.

Adobe InDesign

  1. Make text swatches 100% Black (C0 M0 Y0 K100).
  2. File → Export → Adobe PDF (Print) → Output: Color Conversion: Convert to Destination, Destination: a Gray profile.

Microsoft Word

  1. Set all text to Automatic or pure black.
  2. Change pictures to grayscale: select the picture → Picture Format → Color → Grayscale.
  3. Then convert the saved PDF to grayscale in Acrobat, since Word can't export a grayscale PDF on its own.

Quick answers

Why does my black text count as color?

Most programs save black as RGB, a screen color. When it's printed it becomes a mix of several inks, so the printer sees color in a black and white book.

How do I convert a PDF to grayscale?

In Adobe Acrobat Pro, open Print Production, Convert Colors, and choose a grayscale profile such as Dot Gain 15 percent.
