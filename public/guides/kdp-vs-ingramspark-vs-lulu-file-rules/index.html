KDP vs IngramSpark vs Lulu vs Barnes & Noble Press: print file rules side by side

Updated October 28, 2026 · Amazon KDP · IngramSpark · Barnes & Noble Press · Lulu

Each printer has its own rules. A file built for one can be rejected by another, which is why authors who print in two places get surprised.

1. The error

"It passed KDP, so why did IngramSpark reject it?"

Not an error message, but the question behind many of them.

2. Why the printer rejected it

Each printer sets its own numbers. Here are the main ones, from each printer's own guides:

KDPIngramSparkB&N PressLulu
Paperback cover bleed0.125"0.125"0.125"0.125"
Interior safe margin0.25" outside (0.375" with bleed); inside grows with pages0.5" all sides0.5" (0.75" inside)0.5"; inside grows with pages
Barcode area2" x 1.2"1.75" x 1"2" x 1.2"2" x 1.2"
Page count24 to 82818 to 120048 to 80032 to 800

IngramSpark also limits total ink to 240%, which catches many dark covers and photos.

The biggest trap: KDP lets interior text sit as close as 0.25" to the outside edge, but IngramSpark wants 0.5". A KDP-ready interior often fails IngramSpark's safety-area check.

3. The do-it-yourself fix

If you publish in more than one place, build to the strictest rule:

Interior

  1. Use at least 0.5" margins on every side, plus the bigger inside margin your page count needs.
  2. Make the PDF the exact trim size.
  3. Embed all fonts and keep images at 300 DPI.

Cover

  1. Download a separate template from each printer. Spine widths differ because their paper differs.
  2. Keep 0.125" bleed and leave the barcode area clear for the bigger 2" x 1.2" size.

Color

  1. Keep total ink under 240% so the same files also pass IngramSpark.

Quick answers

Can I use the same files for KDP and IngramSpark?

Often not without changes. IngramSpark needs 0.5 inch interior margins and total ink under 240 percent, and its spine width differs from KDP's, so the cover usually needs its own template.

Which printer has the strictest file rules?

For most books IngramSpark is the strictest, mainly because of its 0.5 inch safety margin and 240 percent ink limit.
