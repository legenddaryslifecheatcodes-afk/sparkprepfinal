What is a proof copy, and should you order one before publishing?

Updated November 25, 2026 · Amazon KDP, IngramSpark

A proof copy is a real printed copy of your book that you check before anyone can buy it. It's the single best way to catch problems a screen can hide. Here's what to know.

What is a proof copy of a book? A proof copy is a printed test copy you order before your book goes on sale, so you can check it on paper. KDP proofs have a "Not for Resale" mark and a barcode with no ISBN. You pay for printing and shipping, earn no royalty, and can't resell them.

Why it's worth ordering

A screen makes everything look fine. Paper doesn't. A proof shows you things you'll only see in your hands:

  • Blurry pictures. Anything under about 300 DPI looks soft in print. (Check first with the free DPI checker.)
  • Words too close to the edge or the middle fold. Hard to read, or cut off.
  • Spine text that slides onto the front or back. Common on thin books.
  • Colors that print darker than on screen.
  • Typos. Many authors find mistakes on paper they missed ten times on screen.

What to check when it arrives

  1. The cover: title, name and spine line up, nothing near the edges.
  2. The size: it matches the trim size you picked.
  3. Every picture: sharp, nothing cut off.
  4. Margins: text isn't lost in the middle fold.
  5. Page numbers, chapter starts and blank pages are where you expect.
  6. Read it. Mark every mistake with a pen.

Quick answers

Do I have to order a proof copy?

No, it's optional on KDP. But it's the best way to see your book exactly as readers will, and most problems are easier to spot on paper.

Can I sell a proof copy?

No. KDP proof copies are marked "Not for Resale" and have a barcode with no ISBN. Order author copies after publishing if you want copies to sell or give away.

Do proof copies earn royalties?

No. You pay for the copy and shipping, and proof or author copy orders don't count as sales in your KDP reports.
