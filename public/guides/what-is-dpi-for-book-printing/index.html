What is DPI? Sharp pictures in your printed book, explained simply

Updated November 18, 2026 · Amazon KDP, IngramSpark and any print-on-demand printer

A picture can look perfect on your phone and come out blurry in print. The reason is DPI. Here is what it means, the one number to remember, and how to check your pictures with a free DPI checker before you upload.

What DPI do I need for a printed book? Use 300 DPI for every picture in a printed book, including the cover. DPI means dots per inch: how many pixels are packed into each printed inch. At 300 DPI, a full 6 x 9 inch page needs a picture about 1,800 x 2,700 pixels. Fewer pixels than that will look soft or blocky in print.

What DPI means

DPI stands for dots per inch. A digital picture is made of tiny squares called pixels. When it is printed, those pixels are spread over paper. DPI tells you how many of them land in each inch. More pixels per inch means a sharper picture. (You will also see PPI, pixels per inch. For books, people use the two words to mean the same thing.)

The one number to remember: 300

Printers want pictures at 300 DPI. Amazon KDP's own help pages say images should be at least 300 PPI for the best results. Below that, pictures start to look soft, and photos with fine detail can look blocky.

Your screen is the trap. A screen shrinks a picture to fit, so even a small picture looks crisp there. On paper it has to cover real inches, and that's when missing pixels show.

How many pixels do you need? Simple math

Multiply the printed size in inches by 300. That's it.

Printed sizePixels you need at 300 DPI
Full page, 5 x 8 inch book1,500 x 2,400
Full page, 6 x 9 inch book1,800 x 2,700
Full page with bleed, 6 x 9 (6.125 x 9.25 inches)about 1,838 x 2,775
Half-page picture, 6 inches wide1,800 pixels wide
Full page, 8.5 x 8.5 inch picture book2,550 x 2,550

Can I just make a small picture bigger?

Not really. Changing the DPI number in a photo app, or stretching a small picture, does not add real detail. It just makes each pixel bigger. The only true fixes are to use the original, larger file (from your camera, illustrator or stock site) or print the picture smaller.

Ebook covers are different

For a Kindle ebook cover, what matters is the pixel size, not DPI. Amazon suggests 2,560 x 1,600 pixels (tall), with a minimum of 1,000 x 625. See every size in one place in the book cover dimensions chart.

Quick answers

Is 72 DPI OK for a printed book?

No. 72 DPI is a screen setting. Printed pictures need about 300 DPI, or they will look blurry. 72 DPI is fine only for things people see on a screen, like an ebook cover thumbnail.

Does DPI matter for black and white books?

Yes. Photos and drawings in a black and white book still need about 300 DPI to print sharp. Plain text doesn't, because your fonts print sharp at any size.
