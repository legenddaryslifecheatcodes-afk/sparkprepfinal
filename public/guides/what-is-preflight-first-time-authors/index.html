New to self-publishing? Why your book needs a preflight check before you upload

Updated November 25, 2026 · For first-time self-publishers

You finished your book. Now Amazon KDP or IngramSpark wants a cover file and a pages file that follow their print rules exactly. Here's what that means, what your options really cost, how long each one takes, and how to get it right the first time.

What is preflight in self-publishing? Preflight means checking your book files before you upload them, so the printer doesn't reject them. It covers your cover and inside pages: bleed, margins, page size, spine width, fonts, image sharpness (300 DPI) and color (CMYK and ink limits). Catching problems first saves days of rejections and re-uploads.

What is preflight?

Before a printer prints your book, a machine checks your files. If one thing is off, the book is sent back to you. Preflight means checking your files yourself before you upload, so that doesn't happen.

The printer checks things most new authors have never heard of:

  • Bleed: your cover art must run a little past the edge, because the printer trims the edge off.
  • Colors: screens make color with light (RGB). Printers use ink (CMYK). Too much ink in one spot (over 240% at IngramSpark) gets rejected.
  • Sharp pictures: pictures need about 300 DPI or they print blurry.
  • Margins: text too close to the edge or the spine fold gets cut off or lost. See the trim size and margin chart.
  • Spine width: it depends on your page count and paper, and every printer has its own number.
  • Fonts: every font must be built into the PDF.

Option 1: Hire freelancers

Most first-time authors hire a cover designer and a formatter for the inside pages. Reedsy's vetted professionals charge a median of about $930 for a cover and about $800 on average for interior formatting (Reedsy's figures). That's about $1,730 for both. Newer designers often charge about $300 to $500 for a cover.

  • Time: often several weeks. You find someone, wait for their schedule, look at drafts and ask for changes, usually more than once.
  • Risk: a file that looks great can still be rejected. Not every freelancer checks bleed, the ink limit or the spine for your exact printer. If it's rejected, you go back, wait again and sometimes pay again.
  • Changes later: a typo, a new page count or a new price on the back means another request.

A good designer is worth it if you want original, hand-made artwork. Just know what you're paying for.

Option 2: Buy a publishing package

Self-publishing companies sell packages that do it all for you. BookBaby, one of the best known, says a professional package with editing, a custom cover, formatting and a print run costs roughly $2,000 to $7,000. Its cover design alone starts at $399, and it says the work typically takes several weeks (BookBaby's pricing page).

Option 3: Do it yourself with free tools

Word and Canva cost nothing, but they don't check printer rules for you. A beginner can't safely fix the 240% ink limit or bleed by hand, and you usually find out only when the rejection email arrives.

Quick answers

What does a preflight check look for?

Bleed on the cover, margins and page size on the inside pages, spine width, fonts built into the PDF, picture sharpness (about 300 DPI) and colors and ink. Use the free manuscript checklist to check the writing side too.

How much does it cost to hire someone to make my book print-ready?

Reedsy's figures put a professional cover at a median of about $930 and interior formatting at about $800 on average, roughly $1,730 for both. BookBaby's full packages cost roughly $2,000 to $7,000.

Do I need preflight if I use KDP?

Yes. Amazon KDP checks every file for size, bleed, margins and picture quality, and sends back files that don't pass. Checking first saves you the back-and-forth.
