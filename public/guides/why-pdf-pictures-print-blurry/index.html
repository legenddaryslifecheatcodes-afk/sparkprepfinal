Why the pictures in your book PDF print blurry, and how to fix it

Updated November 24, 2026 · Any PDF for Amazon KDP, IngramSpark or Lulu

Your pictures looked fine on screen, then the printer flagged them as low resolution. Here's why that happens, the Word setting that quietly shrinks your pictures, and how to check every picture in your PDF for free.

Why are the pictures in my PDF blurry? Usually the pictures are too small for the size they print, or Word shrank them when it saved. Print needs about 300 DPI. In Word, turn on 'Do not compress images in file' (File, Options, Advanced), re-insert the pictures and export again. Then check every picture with a free PDF DPI checker.

First: a PDF doesn't have "a" DPI

Text in a PDF prints sharp at any size, because it's made of shapes, not pixels. Only the pictures have a DPI, and each one can be different. A book can have a perfect cover photo and one blurry chart on page 112. So you're really checking each picture. New to the term? Read what DPI means.

Way 1: The simple math (free)

  1. Find the picture's size in pixels (right-click the original image file, then Properties or Get Info).
  2. Measure how wide it prints on the page, in inches.
  3. Divide: pixels ÷ inches = DPI. A 1,500-pixel-wide picture printed 5 inches wide is 300 DPI. Printed 6 inches wide, it's only 250.

Or skip the math: drop your whole PDF into SparkPrep's free PDF DPI checker. It lists every picture on every page with its real DPI. It runs in your browser and never uploads the file. For the full step-by-step, see how to check the DPI of a PDF.

Way 2: Adobe Acrobat Pro

  1. Open your PDF in Acrobat Pro and go to Print Production, then Output Preview.
  2. In the Preview menu, choose Object Inspector.
  3. Click a picture on the page. The panel shows its resolution in pixels per inch.

This works well, but you have to click every picture on every page, and Acrobat Pro is a paid program.

The Word setting that lowers your DPI

If you made your book in Microsoft Word, Word may have shrunk your pictures without asking. Before you save your PDF:

  • Go to File, Options, Advanced, find Image Size and Quality.
  • Tick Do not compress images in file, and set the default resolution to High fidelity.
  • Then re-insert any pictures that were already shrunk, and save the PDF again. Steps: export a print-ready PDF from Word.

If a picture is too low

Use the original, larger file, or print the picture smaller. Making the number bigger in an app doesn't add detail. More fixes: how to fix low-resolution images. Not sure what size the page is? See the trim size chart.

Quick answers

Can I check the DPI of a PDF for free?

Yes. Drop the PDF into SparkPrep's free PDF DPI checker. It shows the real DPI of every picture on every page, and nothing is uploaded.

What DPI should a PDF be for printing a book?

About 300 DPI for every picture at the size it prints, including the cover. Text doesn't have a DPI and prints sharp at any size.

Why are my PDF pictures blurry when they looked fine in Word?

Word compresses pictures by default when it saves. Turn on 'Do not compress images in file' under File, Options, Advanced, re-insert the pictures, and export the PDF again.
