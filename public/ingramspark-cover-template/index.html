IngramSpark Cover Template: How to Use It Without Getting Rejected

IngramSpark gives you a free cover template for your exact book. Most cover rejections still come from small mistakes using it. Here's how to get it right the first time.

Why use IngramSpark's cover template?

IngramSpark builds a template for your exact book: trim size, page count, paper and binding. It shows where the spine folds, where trimming happens and where the barcode goes. Designing without it is the fastest way to get rejected. This guide shows how to use it and the four mistakes that still get covers sent back.

Step 1: Get your template

Use IngramSpark's free cover template generator (see IngramSpark's help article Cover File Creation). You'll need:

  • Your ISBN (the template is tied to it)
  • Trim size, interior color and paper
  • Binding (paperback, case laminate hardcover or dust jacket) and laminate finish
  • Your exact page count from your final interior PDF

The template comes back as a PDF (or InDesign file) with the trim lines, bleed, spine and barcode area already marked for your book.

Step 3: Avoid the 4 template mistakes that cause rejections

1. Leaving template guides in the final file

The template's pink and blue boxes and labels are only guides. If any of them are still in your exported cover, it gets rejected. Put your artwork on its own layer and hide or delete the template layer before exporting.

2. Using the wrong document size

Your cover PDF must be the template's full size, bleed included. Cropping to the trim line, or exporting just the front cover, gets rejected.

3. A spine that doesn't match the page count

If your page count changes after you make the cover, your spine changes too. Get a new template for the new page count. On hardcovers this matters even more: we've seen a dust jacket rejected with "incorrect spine width — spine should be 0.313" for 78 pages".

4. Spine text that's too big

Spine lettering wider than the spine itself, or sitting on the fold lines, gets rejected as "reduce spine text to fit".

Hardcover and dust jacket templates

Hardcovers need different templates: a case laminate cover wraps around the boards (much larger bleed), and a dust jacket adds front and back flaps. A hardcover-with-jacket book needs three files: interior, case cover and jacket. Never adapt a paperback template for a hardcover.
