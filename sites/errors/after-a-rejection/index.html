Next steps

Your file was rejected. Here's what to do next.

The worst thing to do after a rejection is to fix the one thing it mentioned and upload again straight away. Rejections often come in rounds. Here's how to make the next upload the last one.

Quick answer

After a rejection: 1) look up what the message really means, 2) check the whole file for every problem, not just the one named, 3) fix the original file, 4) re-check the new PDF, including the spine if the page count changed, 5) re-upload and approve the new proof.

Step by step

  1. Read the whole message. IngramSpark emails often list several problems in one block of capital letters. Split them into separate items.
  2. Decode each one. Use the message lookup. Write down the likely cause for each.
  3. Check the whole file, not just what was named. Printers sometimes stop at the first few problems. Run the full preflight checklist or a SparkPrep scan.
  4. Fix the original (Word, Canva, InDesign, Affinity), not the PDF. Fix page size first, then margins, then everything else.
  5. Note your new page count. If it changed, your spine changed. Re-fit the cover.
  6. Re-check the new PDFs before uploading.
  7. Upload both files if the spine changed, even if only the interior was rejected.
  8. Approve the new proof carefully. Look at every page in the online preview, then order a printed proof.

If a freelancer made the files

  • Send them the exact message and ask them to fix it. Many include one round of fixes; ask before you pay again.
  • Ask them to build to your printer's rules, not general ones. Name the printer, paper and page count.
  • Get the original working file (InDesign, Affinity or Canva link), not just the PDF, so you can make small changes later.

How to avoid round two

  • Don't change anything else while fixing. A late edit can move pages and change the spine.
  • Keep both files in step: interior page count and cover spine always match.
  • Keep a note of your printer's numbers for this book: trim, paper, page count, spine width.

How long it costs you

Each round means fixing, uploading and waiting for a new review, often a few days each time. Authors who go round three or four times lose weeks, and sometimes a launch date. Questions people ask

Should I fix the PDF or the original file?

The original file. Editing a PDF directly often creates new problems. Fix the source, then export a new PDF.

Why was my book rejected again after I fixed it?

Usually a second problem that wasn't in the first message, or a fix that changed the page count and so the spine width.
