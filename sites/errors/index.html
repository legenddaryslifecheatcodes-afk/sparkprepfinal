Got a rejection? Start here.

Your book was rejected. Here's what the message means.

Printer rejection messages are short, shouty and written for print shops. This site turns each one into plain English: what it means, why it happened, and what to do next.

Quick answer

Most KDP and IngramSpark rejections come down to a handful of problems: missing bleed, content outside the safety area, a PDF at the wrong page size, total ink over 240%, color in a black and white book, a wrong spine width, fonts not embedded and template lines left on the cover. Each has a known fix.

First, don't panic

A rejection doesn't mean your book is bad, and it doesn't cost you your spot. It means one or two technical things in the file don't match the printer's rules. Almost all of them are fixable in an evening, if you know what they mean. Here's exactly what to do after a rejection.

Why the messages are so confusing

  • They're written for print professionals. "Bookblock", "TAC" and "PDF/X-1a" are print-shop words.
  • One message can have several causes. "Content outside the safety area" can be small margins, low page numbers or the wrong page size.
  • They rarely say where. You get the problem, not the page number.
  • Several problems come at once, and fixing one can trigger another.

Questions people ask

Why did IngramSpark reject my book?

The most common reasons are missing cover bleed, interior content outside the 0.5 inch safety area, total ink over 240%, color in a black and white interior, and a wrong spine width.

Does a rejection cost money?

The rejection itself doesn't. What costs you is time: fixing, re-uploading and waiting for the next review, and paying a freelancer again if they made the file.
