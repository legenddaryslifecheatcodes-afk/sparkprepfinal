Fix: picture quality

How to fix low-resolution pictures

A picture that looks sharp on your screen can print soft or blocky. Printers want 300 DPI at the size the picture prints, and some apps quietly lower it when you save.

Quick answer

A picture needs 300 pixels for every inch it prints. Multiply the printed width and height in inches by 300 to get the pixels you need. If the picture is smaller, use a bigger original, print it smaller, or turn off image compression before exporting.

Stop Word from shrinking your pictures

  1. File → Options → Advanced → Image Size and Quality.
  2. Tick Do not compress images in file and set Default resolution to High fidelity.
  3. Delete the pictures and insert the originals again (pictures already compressed stay compressed).
  4. Save as PDF with "Standard (publishing online and printing)".

Canva

  • Always download PDF Print, never PDF Standard.
  • Upload the biggest version of your photo you have. Don't stretch a small picture to fill a full page.

InDesign

  • Window → Links shows each picture's Effective PPI. Aim for 300 or more.
  • On export, under Compression, don't downsample below 300.

What doesn't work

  • Changing the DPI number in Photoshop without adding pixels. It just relabels the file.
  • Screenshots. A screenshot is screen resolution (about 72 to 150).
  • Pictures saved from social media or messages. They're shrunk on upload. Ask for the original file.

Questions people ask

What DPI do I need for a printed book?

300 DPI at the printed size. Line art and text-only images look best at 600.

Can I print a 72 DPI picture?

Only very small. A picture at 72 DPI prints sharp at about a quarter of its screen size.
