Book preflight, explained simply

What is book preflight?

You wrote the book. Before Amazon KDP or IngramSpark will print it, a machine checks your two files: the cover and the inside pages. Preflight is you checking them first, so nothing gets sent back.

Quick answer

Book preflight is checking a book's cover and interior PDF files against the printer's rules (size, bleed, margins, colors, picture sharpness, fonts and spine width) before uploading, so the printer accepts them the first time.

Why printers send files back

A printed book is not a big printout of your Word file. The printer prints your cover on a larger sheet, trims it, folds it around a spine that is exactly as thick as your pages, and glues it on. The inside pages are printed with ink, not light, and are bound so part of every page disappears into the fold.

Each of those steps has a rule. If your file breaks one, the printer either rejects it or prints something you won't like: a white line on the edge, words cut off near the spine, a muddy photo or a title sliding off the side of the spine.

The six things every preflight checks

1. BleedBackground art has to run 0.125" past every edge of the cover, because that edge is cut off. 2. MarginsText must stay far enough from the edges and the spine fold. IngramSpark wants 0.5" all around. 3. Color and inkPrinters use ink (CMYK), not screen light (RGB). IngramSpark rejects more than 240% total ink. 4. Picture sharpnessPictures need about 300 DPI at the size they print, or they come out soft. 5. FontsEvery font must be built into the PDF, or the printer can't print your words the way you set them. 6. Spine and sizeThe cover must be the exact size for your page count and paper. Every printer measures this differently.

How preflight fits into publishing

  1. Finish your files. A cover PDF and an interior PDF, made at your book's trim size (for example 6" x 9").
  2. Preflight them. Check every rule above, for your printer, for this exact page count and paper.
  3. Fix what failed. Then check again. A fix to one thing (like the page count) can break another (like the spine width).
  4. Upload. The printer runs its own check. If you preflighted well, this is where your book is accepted.
  5. Order a proof copy. Hold the real book in your hands before you hit publish.

Preflight doesn't replace the proof copy. It makes sure you get to the proof copy without a rejection email first. See preflight vs. a proof copy for the difference.

Who needs it

Anyone sending a print book to Amazon KDP, IngramSpark, Barnes & Noble Press or Lulu. First-time authors need it most, because nobody tells you these rules until a file comes back. Seasoned authors need it when they switch printers, change paper, add pages or go from paperback to hardcover. Here's who needs what.

Questions people ask

What does preflight mean in self-publishing?

It means checking your cover and interior files against the printer's technical rules before you upload them, so KDP, IngramSpark, Barnes & Noble Press or Lulu accepts them the first time.

Does Amazon KDP check my files for me?

KDP checks them when you upload and shows problems in its previewer. Preflight means you find and fix those problems before that point, and catch things the previewer only warns about.

Is preflight the same as proofreading?

No. Proofreading checks your words. Preflight checks your files: size, bleed, margins, colors, pictures and fonts.

How long does preflight take?

By hand with a checklist, an evening or two. With SparkPrep, a few minutes for the scan.
