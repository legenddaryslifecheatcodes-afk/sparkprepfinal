KDP vs. IngramSpark: the file rules side by side

Most authors print with KDP, IngramSpark, or both. Their rules look alike but differ in the places that cause rejections. Here are the numbers that matter.

Quick answer

Both need 0.125" bleed and 300 DPI pictures. IngramSpark is stricter: 0.5" safety margins on every page, a 240% total ink limit and a 1.75" x 1" barcode area. KDP allows 0.25" outside margins (0.375" with bleed), grows the inside margin with page count and uses a 2" x 1.2" barcode area.

What this means for you

If you build for IngramSpark, KDP usually accepts it too

Use 0.5" margins on every side (plus the bigger inside margin your page count needs), keep total ink under 240%, and save as PDF/X-1a. That interior will usually pass both. The cover is the exception: it needs its own size for each printer.

Never reuse one cover for both

Because the paper differs, the spine differs. A 300-page book on white paper has a KDP spine of about 0.676" and an IngramSpark spine of about 0.586". That's almost a tenth of an inch, enough to push your spine text onto the front cover. Build two covers from two templates. The ink limit trips up dark covers

A black or deep navy cover made in Canva or Photoshop is often 300% ink or more. KDP may print it; IngramSpark will reject it. How to bring ink under 240%.

Questions people ask

Can I use the same files for KDP and IngramSpark?

The interior often works for both if it's built to IngramSpark's stricter rules. The cover almost never does, because the spine width differs.

Which is stricter, KDP or IngramSpark?

IngramSpark, mainly because of its 0.5 inch safety margin on every page and its 240% total ink limit.
